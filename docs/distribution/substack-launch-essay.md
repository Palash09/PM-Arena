# In the AI era, judgment is the builder's advantage

**Subtitle:** Why I turned real product leadership stories into a playable decision game.

Football and product management have something important in common.

The outcome often turns on a decision made under pressure: incomplete information, limited time, competing objectives, and no option that is obviously correct.

That is part of why I love both.

Today, AI can help builders move from an idea to a working product faster than ever. It can generate interfaces, analyze information, write code, and accelerate execution. But faster execution does not answer the most important questions:

- Which problem is worth solving?
- What should we deliberately not build?
- Which trade-off are we willing to accept?
- What evidence would make us change course?

As building becomes easier, judgment becomes more valuable.

## Knowing is not deciding

Product managers have access to more frameworks, podcasts, newsletters, and case studies than they can realistically use. The shortage is not information.

The shortage is practice.

Reading how a strong product leader handled a difficult situation is useful. Making the call yourself before seeing what happened creates a different kind of learning. It forces you to expose your assumptions, reject an alternative, and defend the trade-off.

That is the idea behind **Product Decision League**.

It is a mobile-first decision game built around real situations discussed by product leaders. Each scenario gives you the company context and a decision point. You choose a path, explain your reasoning, and then compare your call with the real-world approach.

The historical decision is the reference point, not the only acceptable answer. A different approach can still be credible when the reasoning is coherent. The result shows where your answer aligned, where it diverged, and what you may have missed.

## From product conversations to playable scenarios

The scenario library was inspired by the depth of product-leader conversations on **Lenny's Podcast**. Lenny Rachitsky has created an unusually rich body of practical product thinking, and those conversations made it possible to identify decisions worth practicing rather than merely summarizing.

The first public challenge draws from Tomer Cohen's experience shaping the purpose of LinkedIn's feed. The player has to decide whether to optimize the feed as a traffic engine, define it as a professional conversation product, or leave its direction to creators.

It takes about two minutes and requires no login.
